# Decisions

One line per choice not fixed by PLAN.md, with the reason.

- Project root is this folder, not a `factindic/` subfolder: PLAN.md already lives here.
- Model: `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct`, chosen by the student before any results: smallest model with official Hindi support. Fixed for the whole project.
- Data loaded from the auto-converted Parquet branch (`refs/convert/parquet`, `hindi/test/0000.parquet`): the dataset's loading script does not run on `datasets` 5.x.
- Article selection: keep test articles of 150-600 whitespace words (6,414 of 8,847 qualify), shuffle with seed 42, take the first 100. This reconciles "first 100" with "seeded sampling".
- All text is Unicode-normalised with indic-nlp-library's DevanagariNormalizer and whitespace is collapsed before anything else, so the two Unicode spellings of nukta letters (one code point for ज़, or ज plus a combining dot) compare equal.
- **Deviation from PLAN.md section 3 (student's decision after the Day 1 review):** only nouns and adjectives are replaced, not verbs. Real Hinglish brings English verbs in with a Hindi helper ("decide किया"); a bare English word in place of a conjugated Hindi verb gives "उन्होंने said कि" or "prisoner created जाने". Verbs were 13% of eligible words, and that group included है/था ("is"/"was"), function words that slipped through as VERB. Content words come from Stanza's Hindi POS tagger (NOUN, ADJ).
- Any word overlapping a checklist span (entity, number or date) is protected, even when tagged NOUN (e.g. पुलिस inside "महाराष्ट्र पुलिस").
- English equivalents come from the MUSE hi-en dictionary, not the LLM: free, deterministic, no GPU. Singular words are looked up by lemma first and plural words (Stanza `Number=Plur`) by their exact form first: singular फ़ैसले ("इस फ़ैसले के बाद") is spelled like the plural and would otherwise become "decisions".
- When MUSE lists several translations, take the most frequent English word (wordfreq Zipf score); drop it if the score is below 3.0, since such candidates are mostly transliterations (अदालत -> "adalat").
- Words with no usable translation are not eligible, so "L%" means L% of words we can actually replace. Context-free lookup gives some wrong senses (हवाई -> "hawaii", विराम -> "comma"); kept as a stated limitation.
- Replaced words are a prefix of one shuffled order per article (seed = "42-<article id>"), which makes levels nested by construction. Counts use round(L/100 * n).
- Numbers: digit runs (Western or Devanagari) with optional commas or a decimal point, stored as Western digits without commas. Number words (तीन, पाँच) and units (लाख, करोड़) are not converted.
- Dates: dd/mm/yyyy-style, "<day> <month> [year]" and "<month> <year>" with Hindi month names. Digits inside a date are not also counted as numbers. Day names (रविवार) and bare years are not dates (a bare year counts as a number).
- NER: `ai4bharat/IndicNER` (PER/LOC/ORG, score >= 0.5), run one sentence at a time to stay under the 512-token limit.
- IndicNER is built on uncased mBERT, whose tokenizer strips Devanagari vowel signs, and the HF pipeline's own word grouping then produces broken spans. So we take per-token labels (`aggregation_strategy="none"`), give each whitespace word its first sub-word's label, join B-/I- runs ourselves (punctuation ends an entity), and trim punctuation from span edges.
- Entities made up only of function words (के, की, में, ...) are dropped (`config.ENTITY_STOPWORDS`): NER sometimes tags them, and they would inflate recall.
- Dictionary lookup also retries without the nukta dot (फ़ैसला -> फैसला), since BBC Hindi uses nukta and MUSE mostly does not.
- Level L% is L% of eligible words, as PLAN.md defines it. Measured English share of all words in the article: 0 / 4.7 / 9.3 / 14.0 / 18.6 % at levels 0-80 (75 eligible words per article on average, minimum 34). The report must state this so the level labels are not misread.
- Checklist sizes over the 100 articles: 13.5 entities, 2.5 numbers, 0.3 dates on average; 34 articles have no numbers and 82 have no dates (BBC Hindi rarely writes full dates).
- Prompt instructions are in English (summary requested in Hindi, Devanagari): a 1B model follows English instructions much more reliably. Both prompts share the same task sentence; the checklist prompt adds "Keep these key facts from the article exactly as they are written:" with one bullet line per non-empty category. One user message, no custom system prompt.
- Llama 3.2's chat template inserts today's date; it is pinned to `CHAT_DATE = "01 Nov 2026"` so prompts are identical on every run.
- 4-bit = bitsandbytes NF4 with float16 compute (the T4 has no bfloat16). Greedy decoding, `MAX_NEW_TOKENS = 200`, batch size 1 (padding in batches can change greedy outputs slightly). Each row stores `hit_max_tokens` to flag cut-off or looping outputs.
- The dry run (first 10 articles = 100 rows) writes to `results/dry_run.jsonl`, separate from `generations.jsonl`, so it never leaks into the real run if prompts change after review.
- Without CUDA, `generate.py --dry-run` falls back to fp32 on CPU/Apple GPU for code testing only, and the full run refuses to start. Every row records its `setup`, so a non-T4 row can be spotted.
- Resume: rows are appended and fsynced one at a time; on restart a half-written last line is cut off (otherwise the next row gets glued onto it, found by a crash test) and finished (id, level, prompt) keys are skipped.
- GPU run environment (Kaggle, Tesla T4, 4-bit): torch 2.11.0+cu130, transformers 5.22.2, bitsandbytes 0.50.2, accelerate 1.15.0. Full run took 1.8 h (6.5 s per summary); the 100 dry-run rows were reproduced word for word.
- Scoring uses only the first 3 sentences of each summary (`config.SCORE_SENTENCES`; student's choice "A" after the full run): 45% of summaries hit the 200-token cap and about half end mid-sentence, so cutting enforces the prompt's own "2-3 sentences" for everyone. Sentences end at । . ? ! followed by a space, or at a line break. The cap-hit rate is still reported per level and prompt.
- A first line ending in ":" is dropped before cutting: 25 summaries open with a lead-in such as "हिंदी में इस खबर का संक्षेप निम्नलिखित है:" (some echo "2-3 sentence", which would otherwise count as unsupported numbers 2 and 3).
- Fact matching: both texts go through Unicode normalisation, Devanagari -> Western digits, nukta removal, lowercasing and punctuation -> space, then entities and dates must match as whole words (भारत is not found in भारतीय). Numbers are compared as normalised values (13 does not match 130). No transliteration between Latin and Devanagari: summaries are 98-100% Devanagari.
- Recall = recalled checklist facts / all checklist facts, per summary, averaged. Number recall is averaged only over the 66 articles that have numbers; date recall is not reported (only 18 articles have dates).
- Precision = facts found in the summary (IndicNER entities + regex numbers + dates) that also appear in the original level-0 article / all facts found. A number inside a date in the article still supports the same number in the summary. Summaries with no facts have undefined precision; they are left out of the mean and counted in `n_without_facts`.
- Known precision noise (audited after the first scoring run): of 185 unsupported entities, 39 contain Latin letters (NER tagging English words copied from the mixed input, e.g. "अमरीकी embassy", "plea"), and 71 are close spellings of an article phrase (some real name distortions like वारिन्का for वावरिन्का, many just grammar like भारत की/का सुरक्षा परिषद). Unsupported numbers (67) look mostly genuine (e.g. invented years 2023/2024, 3000).
- Length = whitespace words of the scored (cut) summary; compression = that / article words. Raw model output tokens (before cutting) are reported separately.
- Unsupported numbers within a summary are listed in sorted order: they come from a set, and unsorted sets made scores.jsonl differ between runs. With that fix, two runs give byte-identical metrics.csv and scores.jsonl.
- `precision_no_latin` (student's choice, option 1): precision recomputed without "entities" that contain English letters, reported next to `precision`, never instead of it. Correction: a first quick check suggested this explained most of the precision drop, but that check wrongly matched the English letters in the label "entity" itself. The real column moves level-80 plain precision only from 0.923 to 0.930.
- `summary_latin_share` added to metrics.csv so the report's "the model answers in Hindi" claim comes from metrics.csv (at most 1.3% of summary words contain English letters).
- Error tagging: the same 8 seeded articles at levels 0/40/80 x both prompts (48 summaries), so differences between conditions are not differences between articles. The sheet is blind: level and prompt sit in a separate key file, and the order within each article is shuffled. A summary can take several tags, separated by ";". Sheets are never overwritten once they exist.
- Checklist quality check: 20 seeded articles (seed 43, so different from the tagging sample); the student counts wrong and missed facts per article.
- The sheet-making and tag-counting code lives in `score.py` behind `--make-sheets` / `--summarise-tags`, to keep to the plan's four pipeline scripts. CSVs are written with a UTF-8 byte-order mark so Excel/Numbers show Devanagari.
- Report: REPORT.md in the project root.
- Language/script drift tag = the summary contains English words, in Latin or Devanagari script (e.g. "legislators", "फ़ार्मस" for "famous"). Garbled Hindi or a misspelt name is not drift.
- At the student's request, Claude audited both hand-filled sheets. `error_tags_filled.csv` and `checklist_check_filled.csv` are the student's untouched originals. `error_tags.csv` and `checklist_check.csv` are the audited versions; each keeps the student's values in `student_*` columns and explains every change in `audit_note`. The student reviews the changes. The report states that the final tags are AI-revised.
- Error-tag audit: 12 of 48 items changed. Drift was applied consistently (added on 8, 9, 11, 18, 36, 41; removed on 6, 24, 31, which have no English words). Entity swaps were added on 21, 34 and 35. Items 6 and 24 were given "unsupported addition".
- Checklist audit: the student recorded 0 missed facts everywhere; Claude recounted 39 (11 names NER skipped, 28 numbers or dates written as words). Not counted as missed: "एक" used as the article "a", weekdays, a bare month, languages, generic titles, and spelling variants of a listed item. One wrong item added ('गाला'). Result: 17 of 373 items wrong (4.6%); about 95% of items correct and about 90% of facts covered.
- Stretch (targeted vs random mixing), set up at the student's request: *targeted* replaces words inside checklist facts with their English form; *random* replaces the same number of non-fact words, taken as the same prefix of the main experiment's per-article shuffle (so it is nested with the main levels). Count per article = number of usable fact words (mean 11.4, max 29; one article has none, so both of its conditions equal the original).
- A fact word is replaced only when its MUSE English is the Latin spelling of the same name: a rough hand-written romanisation (पाकिस्तान -> pakistan) must match the English with difflib similarity >= 0.55. This keeps Pakistan, Supreme Court, Modi, BJP and rejects literal translations of names (मुलायम -> soft, सिंह -> leo, अरब -> billion, सच्चा सौदा -> true deal) and function words inside spans (के -> of). It also rejects some fine ones (भारत -> india, दिल्ली -> delhi, बीबीसी -> bbc). The indic-nlp ITRANS transliterator was tried first but returned empty strings without its resource files.
- Replaced fact words are capitalised as names; English with no vowels is an acronym and is uppercased (bjp -> BJP). Numbers stay digits, so targeted mixing changes names, places, organisations and month names, not numbers.
- Stretch scoring: a checklist fact counts as recalled in its Hindi form or its English form (the same word-by-word swap), for both conditions. Precision support = the original article plus its targeted version, for both conditions. Caveat: IndicNER may not tag Latin-script names in summaries, so precision for targeted summaries can be biased.
- Stretch generation: 100 articles x 2 conditions x 2 prompts = 400 summaries, same model and settings, `python generate.py --stretch` -> results/stretch_generations.jsonl. The checklist prompt still lists facts in Hindi, even when the targeted article spells them in English.
- Model-size extension (2026-11-10, student's request after the plan was complete): the same pipeline on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, NF4 4-bit on a Kaggle T4, same prompts, greedy decoding and 200-token cap. Lean scope chosen to fit the Kaggle GPU quota: levels 0/40/80 x 2 prompts (600) + the stretch (400) = 1,000 summaries per model. The 1B main run is unchanged and stays the main result; larger models write to results/scale/<tag>/. Caveat: 8B is from the 3.1 release, 1B/3B from 3.2, so size is not the only difference.
- Model-size scoring: `score.py --model 3b/8b [--stretch]` uses the same scoring code; `--compare-sizes` combines 1B (main scores, levels 0/40/80 only) with 3B and 8B. Stretch intervals are now computed in code (`paired_diffs`, seeded, 2,000 resamples); this moved the 1B intervals in REPORT.md section 6 by at most 0.003 from the earlier ad-hoc script. The size chart is small multiples (one column per model, x = mixing level), because model size on the x-axis hid the mixing effect.
- Second model family (2026-11-11, student's choice after Claude's options): Gemma 2 2B and 9B instruction-tuned, chosen because a 2025 Hindi benchmark ranked Gemma-2-9b-it the best small open model and both sizes come from one release (so size is not mixed with release, unlike Llama 8B). Same lean scope. Gemma 2 overflows in float16 and the T4 has no bfloat16, so Gemma runs use float32 compute and float32 unquantised weights (setup `cuda-4bit-fp32`); Llama keeps float16. generate.py now stops if the first 6 summaries contain no Devanagari, to catch broken output early.
- Significance (2026-11-11): `--compare-sizes` also writes size_mixing_diffs.csv, paired bootstrap intervals for level 80 - level 0 and checklist - plain, per model. REPORT.md sections 3, 7 and 8 quote these; levels 20/60 are untested and there is no multiple-comparison correction.
- F1 (2026-11-11, from the mentor's brief): per-summary harmonic mean of checklist recall and fact precision, averaged; NaN when a summary has no facts. Compression ratio and inference time were already computed and are now reported in REPORT.md.
- Automatic meaning check (2026-11-11, brief's 'meaning preservation' objective): sentence-level NLI with MoritzLaurer/mDeBERTa-v3-base-xnli-multilingual-nli-2mil7 (XNLI includes Hindi). Premises are overlapping 4-sentence article windows; the 3 with most word overlap are checked per summary sentence; thresholds 0.5, not tuned. Validated against the 48 final hand tags before running it on all 3,000 summaries (levels 0/40/80, five models). Runs locally on MPS.
- Language scope (2026-11-11): the brief asks for 'any 2' languages; the student decided English + Hindi satisfies it.
- GitHub (2026-11-11): public repo Swapnil-Pramanik/FactIndic at the student's request, matching their other repos (one-line description, no licence file); data/dict (MUSE, downloaded) and FactIndic_colab.zip (rebuildable) are git-ignored.
- Content preservation (2026-11-11, to cover the brief's "content preservation" objective and the standard summarisation metrics a paper needs): `content.py` scores every summary against the human XL-Sum reference summary with ROUGE-1/2/L F1 and BERTScore F1. ROUGE uses a whitespace tokenizer over the fact-matching normalisation, because the default `rouge_score` tokenizer deletes all non-Latin characters; there is no Hindi stemmer. BERTScore uses `xlm-roberta-large` (BERTScore's recommended multilingual model), raw scores without baseline rescaling. Lead-1 and Lead-3 article baselines give scale. Same 3-sentence cut and paired bootstrap as elsewhere.
